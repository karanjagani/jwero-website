# Awareness, reach, journey, answers, trust — where the site stands and what closes the gap

Date: 2026-09-30. Written after the Cloudflare-style rebuild, the product coverage audit,
the simulations and the /start funnel. Facts about the site are counted from the build
(136 pages, 111 FAQ answers, 18 guides, 5 tools, 28 product pages, 27 solution pages,
19 role pages, 14 comparison pages, 15 named customer logos, 3 named founders).

The frame: a jeweller moves through five states — *hears of you → understands you →
believes you → decides → starts*. The site is now strong on the second and fourth. The
first, third and fifth depend on assets only the company can produce.

---

## 1. Awareness with complete clarity

**What the site does now.** One category line everywhere ("The Autonomous Jewellery OS,
run by AI"); every page opens with The Shift (today → gone → real time); the glossary and
llms files make the vocabulary legible to answer engines.

**What still blurs the picture**
- *Two different first sentences.* Home says "Fifty systems become one"; the Organization
  description says "one customer record, one catalogue, one inventory truth". Both true,
  but a jeweller who hears Jwero described by a friend should be able to repeat one
  sentence. Pick the one-liner for word of mouth and put it in the footer, the OG image,
  the WhatsApp auto-reply and the IIJS panel: **"Jwero runs your whole jewellery business
  from one customer record — and the AI does the remembering."**
- *No visual that travels.* The dot-matrix stone is the brand image; it exists only on the
  home page. It should be the OG image, the WhatsApp profile, the standee, the LinkedIn
  banner — one image people recognise before they read.
- *Category education is missing.* "Autonomous Jewellery OS" is new. A single explainer
  page — `/why-an-os` — that answers "what is an operating system for a jewellery business,
  and why is it not a CRM + ERP + WhatsApp tool" would be the page every ad, post and
  founder talk links to. It does not exist; the platform page assumes the reader knows.

## 2. Reach

**Now.** SEO/AEO/GEO plumbing is in place (schema on every page, speakable, search
action, llms-full, glossary, related links). The ceiling is content and distribution, not
markup.

**Highest-yield moves, in order**
1. **Comparison pages are the intent you already own and cannot publish.** 14 pages are
   built and blocked pending verification. "Jwero vs Ornate / Synergics / Marg / WATI" is
   the single largest untapped search bucket in the category. Verify the competitor
   claims (one afternoon with each vendor's public pricing page) and release them.
2. **Hindi and Gujarati.** The trade searches and reads in these languages; the site is
   English-only. Start with four money pages (home, WhatsApp, POS, pricing) in Hindi with
   `hreflang`. Not a translation project — a rewrite by someone who sells to jewellers.
3. **Founder distribution.** Three founders with LinkedIn profiles and no content trail.
   One post a week each, in first person, about one jeweller's real problem — with a link
   to the page that answers it. Karan on the business, Manav on what the product does
   this week, Mahendra on the trade. This is the cheapest reach the company has.
4. **The four calculators as the ad hook.** "What is your dead stock costing you?" is a
   better ad than any feature. Each tool already sends the result to WhatsApp; run ads to
   the tools, not to the home page.
5. **IIJS and trade events.** The `/whatsapp` QR redirect already carries ref codes; print
   a QR per booth panel that lands on the matching simulation (`/products/pos#try-till`),
   not the home page.

## 3. A journey that is clear and assured

**Now.** Persona switch → solution page → product pages → simulation → `/start` or
WhatsApp. Every hero has a reward-style CTA; every page has "You're probably asking".

**Where a jeweller still hesitates**
- *After WhatsApp, what happens?* The site promises "a real person and our AI reply within
  minutes". Nowhere does it show the *next* 30 days. A page — `/how-it-goes` — with the
  literal sequence: message → 15-minute call → pilot on your own data → change-freeze
  agreement → go-live date → first growth report. With who does what and how long each
  takes. Assurance is a timeline, not an adjective.
- *`/start` leads to a signup we have not verified is open.* If it requires an invite,
  the button is a dead end for the most motivated visitor on the site. Confirm, or make
  step 3 "request your workspace" (WhatsApp) until self-serve is live.
- *Price is one message away.* Even a floor ("from ₹X/month for a single store; most
  single stores pay less than their SMS vendor + catalogue app") removes the largest
  reason not to send the first message.
- *The demo form goes to WhatsApp only.* An owner who prefers a call has to write. Add a
  callback option (phone number + "call me between") on `/book-demo`.

## 4. Answering every ICP, role and business

**Now.** 111 FAQ answers, page-level FAQs on 90 pages, 27 solution pages, 19 role pages,
the diamond-trader section, honest-gaps blocks on 25 pages.

**Gaps by audience**
- *Owner:* "What does my accountant do differently?" and "What if I stop paying?" — both
  answered in fragments; neither has a plain, findable answer. Add to the FAQ hub under
  Pricing & contract.
- *Chain owner:* no page shows head-office vs branch permissions as a table. The Runs/Asks
  toggle pattern should be reused as "Head office sets / Branch sets".
- *Manufacturer:* the metal-closure simulation exists; the karigar's own view (Hindi pilot
  screens) is described, not shown. One screenshot, with permission, closes this.
- *Wholesaler / trader:* memo exposure per buyer is described; a "which buyer holds what"
  mock would make it concrete.
- *D2C brand:* "Keep Shopify" is answered; "what happens to my Shopify data if I leave"
  is not.
- *Karigar / cashier / CRM executive:* role pages speak to them, but the CTA ("Show me my
  morning in Jwero") assumes they decide. They do not; they influence. Their CTA should be
  "Send this page to your owner" (WhatsApp share with a pre-filled note).
- *Non-India:* the site is India-first by design; say so on one line on /enterprise and
  /solutions/export-houses rather than letting a Dubai reader discover GST-shaped copy.

## 5. Trust in the brand and the founders

This is the largest remaining gap, and none of it is a design problem.

**What the site does well.** Public roadmap, "what we don't do yet" on 25 pages, verified
customer logos only, no stock testimonials, founders named with LinkedIn links, legal
entity and CIN in the footer, the button that opens the real product inbox.

**What is missing, in the order it moves a jeweller**
1. **Customer voice.** Fifteen named jewellers and not one sentence from any of them. One
   line each, with name and shop, is worth more than everything written by Jwero. The
   component exists (Cloudflare-style tabbed quotes were skipped only for lack of quotes).
2. **Two case studies with a number and a name.** The site itself says "numbered case
   studies come next, and only after the owner verifies the figures". Do two. A single
   store and a chain. Before/after on one lever each (response time; dead stock cleared).
3. **The founders on camera.** A 90-second video per founder — who they are, why
   jewellery, what they will not promise. Embedded on /company and the home page. Trade
   buyers buy from people; the site currently offers three initials in circles.
4. **Founder direct line.** The site says "the founders' WhatsApp desk" on the company
   page. Make it literal: a "Message Manav directly" button with its own ref code, and
   honour it.
5. **Security page proof.** /trust/security says what is not certified (no SOC 2 / ISO).
   Add what *is* verifiable: hosting region, backup cadence, who has production access,
   the date of the last external review — even if the review was informal.
6. **Live status and changelog.** A `/status` page (even manually maintained) and a dated
   changelog on /roadmap. Trust compounds when a site changes visibly every week.
7. **Press and associations.** The LGD Times article and any trade-body memberships exist
   in the product repo's assets; none are on the site. A "seen in / member of" strip is
   cheap and real.
8. **Pricing in public.** Repeated here because it is a trust signal as much as a
   conversion one: hidden prices read as "depends who you are".

## 6. Do first (two weeks, no engineering)

1. Collect 15 one-line quotes from the logo-wall customers; I wire them in a day.
2. Write two case studies with verified numbers.
3. Verify the 14 comparison pages and release them.
4. Confirm `os.jwero.ai/signup` accepts self-serve sign-ups; otherwise change /start step 3.
5. Publish a price floor.
6. Record three founder videos on a phone. No script beyond "why I am doing this".
7. Write `/how-it-goes` — the 30-day timeline — and `/why-an-os`.

## 7. Then (engineering, one week)

Testimonial component; case study template; Hindi variants of four pages with hreflang;
"Send this page to your owner" CTA on role pages; callback option on /book-demo;
head-office/branch permission table on multi-store; status + changelog pages.
